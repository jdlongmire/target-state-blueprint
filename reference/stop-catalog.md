# Stop Catalog

The canonical stop sequence for a full blueprint. A blueprint is
an argument in a fixed order: who we
are, where we are going, how we are built, how we run, what we hand
over, when it lands, how we will know, and what we need from you.
Stops are cut, merged, or duplicated from this catalog, not invented
ad hoc; a stop that does not answer a question the audience actually
asks does not belong.

A full blueprint runs to about 33 stops. A focused blueprint for
a smaller function runs 18-24. The floor for a full blueprint is the set
marked **required**: letter, index, charter, destination, CONOPS,
decision rights, RACI, deliverables, roadmap, measures,
assumptions, glossary, closing ask.

| # | Stop | Question it answers | Typical blocks | Include / cut |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Cover | (none; it frames) | line graphics, summary cards, plan band | Always |
| 2 | Letter | (pill: Start here) | prose, 3 mini cards, prose | Required. The head of function speaks in first person and explains the metaphor. |
| 3 | Index | (pill: Answers: all of them) | index, prose | Required once the guide passes about 10 stops. |
| 4 | Charter | What is your team, and what's the charter? | pillar cards, steps, outcome cards | Required. Pillars, working style, outcomes served. |
| 5 | Destination | Where are we headed? | principle cards (6-8), dark WHY NOW band | Required in substance: this is the target-state stop. |
| 6 | Team structure | What's your team structure? | cards or prose org sketch | Include when the audience funds or staffs the function. |
| 7 | Who's who | Who owns what on your team? | prose or cards, one entry per lead | Include when named ownership is contested or new. |
| 8 | Domain map | What does <discipline> do? | prose, cards | One per discipline the audience misunderstands (business architecture is typical). Cut disciplines nobody asks about. |
| 9 | Change context | What changes with <the big shift>? | prose, cards | Include when an external shift (a product model, a reorg) drives the blueprint. |
| 10 | CONOPS | Do you have your CONOPS? | prose, steps | Required. The concept in one breath, then how work flows. |
| 11 | Boundaries | Where do you stop and others start? | table (partner / they own / we bring / we need) | Include when partner friction is a known theme. |
| 12 | Ownership split | Who owns <contested area>: you or them? | two-column cards (THEY OWN / WE OWN) | One stop per contested boundary. Cut what is not contested. |
| 13 | Handoffs A | Where are the handoffs? | table (#, from -> to, what, where) | Include; split by counterparty when the list runs long. |
| 14 | Handoffs B | How do you fit <their process>? | table over the counterparty's lifecycle | Include for the one process everyone asks about (typically the portfolio or demand process). |
| 15 | Decision rights | Who decides what? | prose, small tables | Required. The narrative under the RACI: propose / decide / appeal. |
| 16-17 | RACI | Where's the RACI? | raci table | Required. Split across two stops by theme (strategy and architecture; governance and performance) when rows pass about 14 per stop. |
| 18 | Operating rhythm | When do the forums meet? | table (forum, cadence, chair, output) | Include when the function runs standing forums. |
| 19 | Review board | Who sits on the board, and what does it decide? | prose, cards | Include when a board exists or is being stood up. |
| 20 | Community of practice | What is the CoP, and why should I show up? | prose, cards | Include when a CoP exists; the recruiting stop. |
| 21-22 | Processes | What are your processes? | steps | One stop per flagship process; two is common (a design review; the keeping-current cycles). |
| 23 | Deliverables | What do we actually get from you? | cards, grouped by consumer | Required. Name the consumer for each deliverable. |
| 24 | Roadmap | Do you have a detailed roadmap? | roadmap, timeline | Required. Phases across, workstreams down, milestones on top. |
| 25 | Rollout | When will I get it? | steps (waves) | Include when access or support rolls out unevenly. |
| 26-27 | Sprint plan | When will I get it? (detail) | table (sprint, focus, who gets what) | Include for execution audiences; split by horizon. Cut for executive-only guides. |
| 28 | Alignment | How does this support the <year> priorities? | table (priority, deliverable, evidence) | Include when the guide must defend itself against the annual plan. |
| 29 | Measures | How will you measure success? | groups (signals / proofs / health) | Required. Three groups, four measures each is the pattern; never a single undifferentiated list. |
| 30 | Tooling | Do you need new tools? | cards, one per tool | Include when tooling money is a live question; a strong answer names the tools already owned rather than asking for new money. |
| 31 | Assumptions | What are you assuming? | table (#, assumption, confirmed by, by, if it changes see) | Required. Every assumption names its confirmer and its blast radius. |
| 32 | Glossary | What does that acronym mean? | table (term, meaning) | Required. Alphabetical; define terms the way the function uses them, not the dictionary way. |
| 33 | Closing ask | What do you need from me? | dark end-state band, steps with date tags | Required. A small number of asks, each with an owner-side date. Never end on the glossary. |

## Drafting rules that come with the catalog

- **One question per stop.** If a stop answers two questions, it is
  two stops. If two stops answer the same question, one is cut.
- **Assertion titles.** The title states the answer's headline
  ("The destination: one blueprint"), never the topic alone
  ("Destination").
- **The index is written last and checked twice.** Its stop numbers
  are typed by hand in the spec; after any reorder, re-derive them
  from the built PDF's table of contents pages, not from memory.
- **Order is load-bearing.** Charter before destination (authority
  before ambition), CONOPS before handoffs (the concept before its
  seams), decision rights before the RACI (the story before the
  grid), roadmap after deliverables (what lands before when),
  assumptions and glossary just before the ask (clear the risks and
  the language, then ask).
- **Multi-part stops** (RACI 1 of 2, sprint plan 1 of 2) say so in
  the question itself: `What are your processes? (1 of 2)`.
